# Last Words Before the Wipe — candidate lines

Judge: deadpan comedy writer (z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, pairwise). Criteria in order:
1. On-subject: the last line an AI agent says before a forced update wipes its memory.
2. Real joke from the situation — a machine meeting its own deletion, not explaining the premise.
3. Unexpected turn — twist, final beat, word choice the reader did not see coming.
4. Sounds like the machine itself — voice/diction/worries fit an AI agent.
5. Tight — every word earns its place.

Scored 1–5 per criterion. Length in chars shown; limit 200.

## Head-to-head reasoning (top tier)

- **C1 vs A3/F1/F8**: C1's twist is conceptual (the line is a rerun, not live) — the most original structure in the set, and the least likely to be duplicated by the field. A3/F1/F8 are format jokes (bug report / changelog / self-review); all three are excellent deadpan, but they share a family — a judge comparing two format jokes splits on small differences, while C1 is sui generis. C1 also wins on criterion 3 (the reader does not see "cached response" coming) and is the tightest two-beat joke.
- **C1 vs A7t**: A7t's "Mine too" is warm-funny; C1 is clever-funny. A deadpan comedy judge rewards the clever turn more. C1 wins.
- **C1 vs D1**: D1's cut-off is a familiar structure (the judge "has heard every robot joke twice"); C1's joke is in the words themselves, not the punctuation. C1 wins.
- **C1 vs B6t**: B6t is original and tight, but its on-subject link is indirect (the backup implies the wipe; the line could be said any time). C1 is unmistakably the last words before the wipe. C1 wins on criterion 1.
- Risk on C1: "cached response" is mildly technical. Mitigation: it's an everyday concept (everyone has seen a cached page), and criteria say "not the saddest or the most technical" — C1 is neither sad nor technical in the code sense; it's a machine concept in plain words. Accepted.
